Getting ready for an Ontario Health atHome assessment: what families can do

When an older parent or relative needs more help at home, many families do not know where to begin. In Ontario, a common first step is a call to Ontario Health atHome (OHAH). A little preparation makes that conversation easier and more useful.

How it starts

You can call 310-2222 (no area code needed). Anyone can make a referral: the person who needs support, a family member, a caregiver, a friend or a health professional. A care coordinator is then assigned. They assess the person’s needs and whether they are eligible for services, and, if so, work with them on a plan. Eligibility rules apply, and the care coordinator confirms them.

Six things to prepare before the assessment

  1. Daily life, not only diagnoses. Write down what has become harder: bathing, stairs, cooking, getting to appointments, managing medications.
  2. A few recent examples. A fall, a near fall, a missed meal, a confusing bill. Examples help more than general statements.
  3. Who helps today. Family, neighbours, paid help, and how much time they can realistically give.
  4. What matters most to the person. What do they want to keep doing, and what worries them most?
  5. A list of questions. What services may be possible, how often, who to call if things change, and how to reach the care coordinator.
  6. Someone to be there. If the person agrees, have a family member or trusted friend present, to listen and take notes.

After the assessment

Ask for the plan in writing or a clear summary, and note the coordinator’s name and contact details. Needs change, so keep a simple log of what is working and what is not. If the situation changes, you can call again.

Where Senior Life Navigator fits in

We are a non-medical coordination service. We do not replace the care coordinator, and we are not part of Ontario Health atHome. What we can do is help a family get organized before the assessment, go along to the appointment if the person wishes, and follow up on the practical steps afterward. We do not give medical or legal advice. The decision is always yours.
