Phone Scams Targeting Seniors in Canada: 5 Warning Signs and What to Do Next

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC) received more than 112,000 fraud reports in 2025, with reported losses above $704 million. Older adults carry a large share of that harm. In the CAFC’s 2024 statistical report, people aged 60 and over accounted for 27.9% of the dollars reported lost that year, out of a total of about $643.7 million. The same report notes that people over 50 tend to lose more money per fraud than younger people.

For context, 19.0% of Canadians (7.0 million people) were 65 or older at the 2021 Census, and 861,000 were 85 or older.

How to spot a grandparent scam or emergency phone scam: 5 warning signs

These come from how the CAFC describes emergency and grandparent scams, put in our own words.

  1. Pressure and threats. The caller demands action right away. Some say a “gag order” stops you from talking to anyone about it.
  2. Someone you trust, or someone with authority. The caller claims to be a loved one, or a police officer or lawyer acting for them. Typical stories are bail after an arrest, a car accident, hospital or ambulance fees, or being stuck abroad.
  3. Unusual payment. Cash that is picked up in person or sent by mail, cryptocurrency, or gift cards in the text and email versions. The CAFC points out that bail in Canada cannot be paid with cash or cryptocurrency.
  4. A new number or a broken phone. A message from a “family member” says their phone is damaged, gives a new number, and then asks for money.
  5. Caller ID that looks right. Numbers can be faked, so caller ID is not proof of who is calling. The grandparent version often reaches people on a home landline.

What to do if you get a suspicious call

  • Hang up and call your family member using a number you already have, not one the caller gave you.
  • If the caller says they are police, hang up and look up your local police number from a reliable source.
  • Think about what you share on social media and dating sites. Scammers use those details to target people and their relatives.

How to report a scam or fraud in Canada

Contact your local police, and report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC) online at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca (you may report anonymously) or by phone at 1-888-495-8501, Monday to Friday, 10 am to 4:45 pm Eastern, closed on holidays.

What to do if you already sent money to a scammer

Do not be embarrassed. The CAFC asks victims to contact their local police as soon as possible and to report to the CAFC as well.
