Five signs of a phone scam, and what to do next

A phone rings. The caller sounds worried, or official, or very friendly. Within a minute you are asked to act fast. This is how most phone scams work, and they work on smart, careful people too. Here are five signs to listen for, and what to do next.

Five signs

  1. You are told to act right now. Scammers want you to decide before you can think or talk to anyone. A real bank, lawyer or agency will give you time.
  2. You are asked to keep it secret. “Do not tell your family” or “do not tell the bank” is a warning sign. Honest callers do not mind if you check.
  3. The payment is unusual. Gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, or someone coming to your door to collect cash are all common in scams.
  4. The caller claims to be someone you trust. This can be a grandchild in trouble, a police officer, a lawyer, or a government worker. The RCMP reminds us that police and court agencies in Canada will never phone to demand cash or any other form of payment.
  5. They want personal or financial details. Do not share account numbers, passwords, codes sent to your phone, or your SIN with someone who called you.

What to do next

  • Hang up. You do not owe a stranger politeness. Hanging up is safe and it works.
  • Check for yourself. If the call says a family member is in trouble, hang up and call that person directly, using a number you already know.
  • Talk to someone you trust before you do anything, even if the caller says not to.
  • Report it, even if you lost nothing. Call the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 or report online at antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca, and tell your local police.

If you already sent money

Do not be embarrassed. It happens to many people. Contact your bank right away, then report it as above. The sooner you act, the better.
